01  Getting in

There is one sign-in door for the whole site. What you see after it depends on what your account is, not on which address you typed.

1.1The one door

  1. Go to iitgtic.itsjeu.com/login.
  2. Enter your email and password. Complete the bot check if it appears — it is Cloudflare Turnstile and usually passes without a click.
  3. Staff land on Overview in /tic-admin; founders land in /founder.

1.2Your session

Signing in issues a signed, httpOnly session cookie good for thirty days, renewed every time you open the console — you are not signed out while you are using it. It cannot be read by anything running in the page, and every change you make is recorded against your name. Sign out from the menu at the top-right. Every staff sign-in also sends an alert to the operating inbox.

The system checks your account on every request, so a deactivation, a deletion or a role change applies immediately, not at the next sign-in.

1.3The very first admin

On an empty installation, /tic-admin/login creates admin number one from the bootstrap password in the server’s environment. After that it stops granting access for good, and every further account is created in Users (§10).

If the setup form appears and admins already exist

The environment is wrong, not the database — a key from the wrong project, a typo in the project URL, an unreachable host. The console names the failure and refuses to bootstrap while it cannot read the accounts. Do not try to “set it up again”. Call the maintainers (§22).

2.2Conventions you will meet everywhere

The screen never blanksEvery section is rendered by the server; the current screen stays until the next is ready. Toasts confirmA short message after every action — green for done, red for refused. No toast means it did not run. Destructive things ask firstA proper dialog names exactly what will go. Deleting an account asks you to type to confirm. A refusal needs a reasonAnywhere you send something back, the reason box is compulsory — the other person reads it. Passing on asks firstEvery hand-off in the review chain confirms, because it emails people and cannot be pulled back. Private files are never public linksDocuments and resumes open through links that expire after ten minutes. Popovers floatMenus, tooltips and hover cards sit above everything, never clipped inside a panel.

2.3A suggested daily round

  1. Overview. Anything with a number on it is work waiting for you.
  2. Applications. Score and pass on what is at your step. The chain only moves when each person does their part.
  3. Evaluation (admin, coordinators). Set up or attend screening calls; submit scores.
  4. Approvals. Clear team members and detail changes.
  5. Job responses. Read new applications to TIC roles.
  6. Email, weekly. Glance at the usage meter and the Failed tab.

06  Applications

The eight-step incubation application a founder fills in per startup — 38 questions and four documents — and the six-step review chain it travels before the startup is incubated.

6.1The review chain

StepWho has itWhat happens
0AdminAdmin checks it, scores every step, then Pass to CEO. The applicant is emailed that it is under review.
1–2CEOThe CEO reviews and scores independently, then chooses the coordinators who will screen it. Only those coordinators will ever see it.
3CoordinatorsThe screening call on Google Meet (§7). Each coordinator scores the startup on eight criteria.
4CEOThe CEO rechecks the coordinators’ notes and chooses the TIC heads.
5TIC headsEach assigned head scores and Signs off. When every head has signed off, it returns to admin.
—AdminAccept and send final email: the applicant gets the acceptance with the panel’s average scores, and the company is verified.
6AdminMark live: the startup is listed among the incubated startups and the founder is told.

The coloured dots on each row show where an application stands; a rejected one shows the step it stopped at. Each person who must act next is emailed when it reaches them.

6.2Scores

Every scored step of the application — the seven content steps and the documents — takes a mark out of 100 from each reviewer. The pass-on button stays locked until you have scored every step. Reviewers score independently: nobody sees another person’s marks while they are scoring. Scores are averaged into the decision emails.

6.3The review panel

Message to applicantGoes into the email the founder receives. Write it as if they will read only this sentence. Internal noteStays in the console, for the team. RejectAdmin or the CEO can reject at any step; the decline email carries your message. ReopenAdmin only: brings a rejected application back under review. Delete applicationRemoves the record and its documents. Asks first. Irreversible — reject rather than delete unless it is a duplicate or a test.
Every hand-off is final

Passing on emails the next person and cannot be pulled back. The confirmation dialog says exactly who is told. Read it.

Documents open through ten-minute signed links. A document never uploaded reads File missing.

Done  Chain, per-role visibility, scores with gates, assignments, emails at every step.

07  Evaluation

The coordinators’ screening call — step 3 of the chain. A real Google Meet, a structured scorecard, and marks nobody else can see.

7.1Admin

  1. Paste the Google Meet link and pick the date and time, then save. The founder gets an invite and each assigned coordinator is emailed the meeting.
  2. After the call, once every coordinator has submitted, end the meeting. The application goes back to the CEO for the recheck.
  3. Admin sees every coordinator’s scores and the history of every call that has ended.

7.2Coordinators

Join the call from your card, then score each criterion out of 10 with an optional remark:

CriterionLook for
TeamFounder–market fit, complementary skills, commitment, track record.
ProblemHow painful and frequent; who has it; how they cope today.
Solution & productDoes it solve it; the insight; what makes it hard to copy.
MarketSize, growth, first customer segment, why now.
TractionUsers, revenue, pilots, LOIs — evidence over promises.
Business modelHow it makes money, unit economics, sustainability.
Technology & innovationTechnical depth, IP potential, can they build it.
Coachability & TIC fitHonesty under questioning, openness, what TIC adds.

Scores save as you go. Submit scores locks them. Coordinators never see each other’s marks.

08  Job postings

Every role on the Opportunities board, in two tabs. Roles go live the moment they are posted — there is no approval queue.

8.1The TIC tab — the centre’s own roles

  1. Post a job opens the form: title, type (Full-time, Part-time, Internship), work mode (On-site, Hybrid, Remote), location, sector, pay or stipend, closing date, application limit, description.
  2. Post it. It is live on /opportunities/tic-jobs immediately, as IITG TIC.
  3. The row shows responses received out of the limit, and the storage their resumes use. The count opens those responses (§9).
  4. Edit, Close (stop taking applications, keep the responses), Reopen, or Delete.

8.2The Incubatees tab — every startup’s roles

Read-only. You see each role, the startup, its status, and how many applied — never who. A startup’s applicants are the startup’s. Details shows the full role.

To take a role down, open Details, write the reason, and press Remove role. It leaves the board at once, the founder sees “Removed by TIC” with your reason in their console, and they are emailed it. Applicants already received stay with the startup.

8.3When a role stops taking applications

ReasonWhat happens
ClosedIts poster closed it. Can be reopened.
FullIt reached its application limit (200 at most). It closes itself; it cannot be reopened while full.
Past closing dateIt leaves the board by itself on that date.
RemovedTIC removed a startup’s role. Only TIC’s decision; the founder cannot edit or reopen it.

8.4Storage guardrails

TIC hosts every resume, so the board keeps itself small:

  • PDF only, 2 MB. The server checks the file’s bytes, not just its name.
  • 200 applications per role at most, lower if the poster sets it.
  • Resumes are deleted 90 days after a role ends. The poster is emailed a week before, and Download resumes saves them all as one zip. Names and answers are kept.

Done

10  Users and Roles

Every account on the system. The only place a staff account is created.

10.1Users

Tabs: All · TIC staff · Founders · Never signed in · Deactivated · Deleted. “Never signed in” usually means a typo in an email address.

New accountName, email, role and a starting password, with a strength meter. Hand the password over by a channel the person already controls. Edit accountChange details or role, or set a new password — within your rank. DeactivateBlocks sign-in without deleting anything. Reversible. The right first move for a compromised account. DeleteType to confirm. The account owner is emailed; the deletion is kept in the Deleted tab. View asDevelopers only: open the console exactly as another account sees it. A banner shows it; Exit returns you.
Rank protects accounts

A developer manages anyone; an admin anyone but a developer; a TIC Admin other TIC Admins and below. Nobody can reset the password of, or demote, an account ranked above their own. Nobody can act on their own row.

10.2Roles

A read-only matrix of which role opens which section, printed live from the code (§1.4). Changing access is a code change — on purpose, so it is reviewed.

11  Activity

What changed in the data, and what people looked at on the site.

11.1The audit log

One entry per change to a company, job, application or profile — who, what, when, and a Field / Before / After table. It is written by database triggers, so a change made any way at all — console, script, SQL editor — is recorded the same. Console actions name the person.

11.2Page impressions

A card per public page over All, 30 days or 7 days: impressions, unique human visitors, and bot hits. Crawlers are separated by user agent, so the human figure is the one to quote. Console views are flagged so staff clicking does not inflate public numbers.

Done

12  Content

Every word and image on the public site, edited here. No developer, no deploy.

  1. Open a section from the Content index. The editor is built from that section’s own data — text, long copy, reorderable lists, nested groups.
  2. Edit. Nothing is written while you type.
  3. Save changes. The public site shows it immediately. The button reads Saved when nothing is outstanding.

Any image field has a preview and Upload image (5 MB, images only, admin-only bucket). Reset to default copy restores the shipped text. Every save is audited with a before and after, and a page’s search description is read from its own opening copy.

The site cannot be taken down by a bad save

A section that cannot be read falls back to the copy bundled with the site. Job roles are no longer content — they live in Job postings (§8).

19  The security model

WhoReadsWrites
VisitorPublic pages; open rolesOne application per role per email, bot-checked and rate-limited
FounderTheir own startups, application and applicantsTheir own records; roles go live, everything else waits for TIC
StaffWhat their role opens (§1.4); in the chain, only what has reached them or was assigned to themWithin their role; every change audited

What the system guarantees

  • Every table refuses access by default; each has explicit rules. The bypass key exists only on the server.
  • Access is checked on every request, so deactivation and role changes apply at once.
  • Every staff write carries your identity; the audit log names a person.
  • Private documents only open through ten-minute signed links. Resumes are PDF-checked by their bytes.
  • Public forms are bot-checked on the server and rate-limited per address.
  • TIC cannot read a startup’s applicants, anywhere — including through the assistant.
  • Delivery feedback is signature-verified and cannot be replayed. The clean-up job refuses to run without its secret.

What it does not do for you

  • It cannot undo a send, or recover a deleted account, application or file.
  • It does not review what startups publish — roles go live at once; removal is your safeguard.
  • It does not stop a full admin being careless. Be careful whom you give Admin.

22  For the technical caretaker

22.1Commands

pnpm doctorChecks every setting, connects to the project, counts admins and content sections. Run it first when anything looks wrong.
pnpm backupSchema, data, every table as NDJSON, and every stored file.
pnpm restore <dir> --dry-runThe drill: verifies the backup and reports what would be written. Writes nothing.
pnpm check · pnpm lint · pnpm testType-check, lint and tests before a deploy.

22.2Deploys and database changes

Hosted on Vercel; a push to main deploys live. Database changes are SQL files in supabase/migrations, applied in order. Apply a migration before pushing the code that needs it, or the live site breaks until it is applied.

22.3Scheduled job

vercel.json runs /api/cron/resume-retention daily at 03:00 IST: it warns posters a week before and clears resumes 90 days after a role ends. It refuses to run unless CRON_SECRET is set. On the VPS, call the same URL from the server’s cron with that secret as a bearer token.

22.4Secrets, in one line each

  • The database bypass key lives only on the server; the build fails if it reaches a browser.
  • Rotating the session secret signs everyone out — the fast answer to a compromised session.
  • The bootstrap password stops working once the first admin exists.
  • Set ADMIN_ALERT_EMAIL to a real TIC inbox so sign-in alerts, new founders and TIC job responses reach the team.
